In April 2027, I will start my Master's degree at Kyoto University's Graduate School of Informatics, joining the Natural Language Processing group (Language Media Processing Laboratory).

Coming from a Software Engineering background at Nanjing University, I am starting this chapter with an honest admission: **I still know very little about AI research.**

## Why Choose Direction Over Inertia

During admissions, I was fortunate to receive another strong offer in low-level networking and systems. On paper, it was tempting. Yet after looking closely at what I would spend two years waking up to debug, I realized that **interest compounds faster than prestige**.

I want to build and understand intelligent systems — specifically large language models, post-training algorithms, and the engineering stack that makes them fast and reliable in the real world. Joining one of Japan's strongest NLP labs gives me both the academic depth and the freedom to combine my software engineering instincts with modern AI algorithms.

## Why Write Everything in English and in Public?

This website is not meant to look polished on day one. It exists for three practical reasons:

1. **Clarity of thought**: If I cannot explain RoPE, DPO, or an $O(1)$ cache invariant in crisp English prose, I do not truly understand it yet.
2. **Proof of trajectory**: Starting late in AI is fine as long as the learning slope is steep and verifiable. Every commit, experiment note, and milestone on the [Timeline](/timeline) is proof of work.
3. **Staying grounded**: Between research prep, Japanese listening/speaking drills, and recruiting timelines in Japan, it is easy to feel scattered. Keeping a public [Now & Plan](/roadmap) page turns anxiety into a concrete checklist.

Here's to the next four months before Kyoto — one commit, one paper, and one problem at a time.
